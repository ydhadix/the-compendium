## Ship Combat Rules

Ship combat begins when a ship becomes aware of a threat, such as a hostile ship or leviathan-sized creature.

## Ship Officers

Ships have three officer roles: Quartermaster, Pilot, and Spelljammer. Each officer controls different parts of the ship and has access to distinct officer actions.

Most of an adventurer's normal features aren't effective at the scale ships operate at. Nonetheless, an officer can use their officer action to instead take one regular action, bonus action, or cast a spell with a casting time of 1 minute or less. Officers still have their reaction, as normal.

Characters who don't have an officer role act as passengers or crew, and do not take turns during ship combat.

#### Changing Roles

Officer roles are chosen before combat begins and usually don't change during an encounter. However, roles may change mid-combat if circumstances demand it.

An officer can use their officer action to take over another officer's role if the other officer is willing or Incapacitated.

When an officer role becomes vacant — either because its former occupant has switched roles or can no longer perform their duties — any character without an officer role may assume the vacant role immediately (no action required).

## Ship Initiative

Each ship in an encounter has its own initiative, which is shared by its crew and passengers. When a ship rolls initiative, one of its officers rolls for the ship, using that officer's normal initiative bonus.

On the ship's turn, one of the ship's officers acts on behalf of the ship. After that officer takes an action, initiative passes to the next ship in initiative order.

At the end of the last ship's turn, the round does not end. Instead, return to the top of initiative. Each ship takes an additional turn, choosing a different officer to act.

This process repeats until every officer of every ship has taken at least one turn this round. If a ship has fewer officers than another, the ship still acts when its initiative comes up, assigning additional turns to its officers as needed.

The round ends after all officers have taken at least one turn, and the last combatant in initiative order finishes their turn. At that point, the next round begins normally.

#### Condition Durations

While a ship is affected by a status effect that prevents actions (such as Incapacitated), an officer is still chosen on the ship's turns, but that officer forgoes taking any action.

#### Time Scaling

Each round of ship combat represents roughly 1 minute of time. The extended timescale reflects the rhythm of ship-to-ship engagements, where movement, coordination, and timing matter more than moment-to-moment reactions.

## Engagement Ranges

The distance between ships is described broadly in four engagement ranges: Close, Long, Extreme, and Retreat.

Most encounters begin at Extreme range. At this distance, combatants are typically aware of one another but cannot effectively target each other with shipboard weapons.

Retreat range is even further than Extreme range. At the end of each round, if a ship is at retreat range with all enemies, it escapes the combat.

Long range is the distance most ships prefer to engage at, unless they are specifically designed for Close range combat.

Close range is point-blank engagement — combat at this distance is risky, as escaping isn't always an option.

#### Distance Scaling

Engagement ranges are not measured in feet, squares, or tiles; ships in the astral sea are massive entities traveling vast distances. Ordinary creatures' attacks rarely exceed Close range and are generally irrelevant until boarding occurs.

## Damage and Hit Points

Each ship is treated as a single combatant with its own Armor Class (AC), Damage Threshold (DT), and two types of hit points: Hull and Keel.

Hull Points (HP) represent a ship's external defenses and can be repaired readily, even during combat. When a ship's HP are reduced to 0, it is Incapacitated but not destroyed. At that point, the ship can be boarded, and its Keel Points can be targeted.

Keel Points (KP) represent the ship's structural integrity. A ship has significantly fewer KP, which cannot be restored without powerful magic or extensive downtime. When a ship's KP are reduced to 0, the ship is destroyed, its life support systems fail, and its crew are in mortal danger.

#### Damage Scaling

Ships can endure significantly more damage than individual creatures, and their weapons' destructive power reflects this. Damage dealt to a ship from a non-ship source is halved, and damage dealt by a ship to a non-ship is doubled.

## Leviathan-class Creatures

Some creatures grow so large that they rival Spelljamming ships in size. These creatures are called Leviathans.

In combat, Leviathans follow the same rules and are treated as ships, except they cannot be boarded.

Instead of officers, each Leviathan has a set number of actions it takes each round. On each of the Leviathan's turns, it performs one action, just as a ship would, and typically cannot take that action again in the same round.

If a Leviathan uses an ability that requires multiple actions to complete, it must spend each of its turns Concentrating on that action. It cannot act again until the final required turn is reached, at which point the action resolves.
