# Ship Encounters
Combat between ships is fought Turn by Turn as each crew's Officers steer, fire, and work their magic in Initiative order.
{ .subtitle }

## Ship Initiative
Ship Encounters begin when a Ship becomes aware of a threat, such as an Enemy Ship or a Leviathan-sized creature.  When a Ship rolls Initiative, one of its Officers rolls for it using that Officer's Initiative bonus. The result is shared by the Ship's crew and passengers.

Each Ship in an encounter gets at least one Turn each Round for each Officer aboard it.  Ship Turns are taken in Initiative order, alternating between each Ship and choosing a different Officer to act on the Ship's behalf each Turn.  A Ship with fewer Officers than another still acts when its Initiative comes up, assigning extra Turns to its Officers as needed.

The Round ends once every Officer has taken at least one Turn and each Ship has taken the same number of Turns. The next Round then begins normally.

#### Ship Actions
On each of a Ship's turns, one of the Ship's Officers are chosen to take an Action on the Ship's behalf.  Each Officer has a different set of **Ship Actions** available to them.

A character without an Officer role acts as a passenger or crew and doesn't take Turns during Ship Combat.

Most of an adventurer's normal features are ineffective at the scale Ships operate. Even so, an Officer can spend their Ship Action to instead take one Normal Action or Bonus Action, or to cast a Spell with a Casting Time of **1** minute or less. Officers keep their Reaction, as normal.

###### Prevented Actions
While a Ship is affected by an effect that prevents Actions, such as being Incapacitated, an Officer is still chosen on the Ship's Turns, but that Officer takes no Action.

## Encounter Scale
Time and distance in a Ship Encounter aren't strictly measured in minutes, feet, squares, or tiles; Ships in the astral sea travel vast distances, and move faster than ships their size could in atmosphere.

#### Time
Time during Ship Combat is tracked in Rounds, which can be as quick as a few seconds or as long as a few minutes.  For resolving creature-scale effects, treat **1** Round as **1** minute.  The longer timescale reflects the rhythm of ship-to-ship engagements, where movement and coordination matter more than split-second reactions.

#### Distance
The distance between Ships is tracked in four broad **Engagement Ranges**: Close, Long, Extreme, and Retreat.  Most encounters begin at Extreme range, where combatants are aware of one another but can't yet effectively target each other with shipboard Weapons.

| Engagement Range | Description |
|---|---|
| Close | Point-blank engagement. Combat here is risky, since escape isn't always an option. |
| Long | The distance most Ships prefer to engage at, unless they are built for Close-range combat. |
| Extreme | Where most encounters open, beyond the effective reach of shipboard Weapons. |
| Retreat | Beyond Extreme range. At the end of each Round, a Ship at Retreat range from all its Enemies escapes the combat. |

An ordinary creature's Attacks rarely reach beyond Close range and matter little until boarding occurs.

#### Damage
Ships endure far more damage than individual creatures, and their Weapons strike accordingly.  Damage dealt to a Ship from a non-Ship source is halved, and damage dealt by a Ship to a non-Ship is doubled.

## Leviathans
**Leviathans** are colossal creatures of the void so vast they're treated as ships, fighting with their own Actions rather than a crew.

A Leviathan follows the same rules as a Ship and is treated as one.

#### Leviathan Actions
Instead of Officers, a Leviathan has a set number of Actions it takes each Round. On each of its Turns, it takes one of those Actions, just as a Ship would, and typically can't take that Action again in the same Round.

If a Leviathan uses an ability that takes multiple Actions to complete, it spends each of its Turns Concentrating on that ability. It can't act again until the final required Turn, when the ability resolves.
