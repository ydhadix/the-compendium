# Creating a Ship
This page details building a vessel from the ground up, choosing its size, hull, defenses, and the components its crew will command.
{ .subtitle }

### Ship Size
A Ship's size is measured by its length and width, in feet.  For every 30 feet of the Ship's longest dimension (rounded up), its **Size Class** increases by **1**.

#### Size Penalty
Before a Ship's Pilot makes a Piloting Check, reduce the total by the Ship's Size Class.  This reduction is the Ship's **Size Penalty**.

#### Ram Damage
Whenever a Ship's Pilot uses the Ram Action, its **Ram Damage** adds **2d10** Bludgeoning Damage for each point of Size Class the Ship has.

### Hull Material
Most Ships are made from Wood or an equivalent substance.  Alternative materials can affect a Ship's defenses and mobility.

### Defenses
A Ship's defensive capability is measured by its Armor Class, Damage Threshold, Hull Points, and Keel Points.

#### Hull Points
**Hull Points** (HP) represent a Ship's external defenses and can be repaired readily, even during combat. When a Ship's Hull Points reach **0**, it is Incapacitated but not destroyed. It can then be boarded, and its Keel Points can be targeted.

#### Keel Points
**Keel Points** (KP) represent a Ship's structural integrity. A Ship has far fewer Keel Points, and they can't be restored without powerful magic or extensive downtime. When a Ship's Keel Points reach **0**, the Ship is destroyed, its life support fails, and its crew are in mortal danger.
