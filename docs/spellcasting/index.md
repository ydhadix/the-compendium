## Spellcasting Rules

## Schools of Magic
Every spell belongs to a school of magic.  These categories generally describe what spells can do, and are used to reference groups of spells, but have no specific rules of their own.

Most spells belong to only one school of magic, though powerful or complex spells may belong to multiple schools.

| **School** | Typical Effects |
|---|---|
| **abjuration** | prevent or reverse harmful effects |
| **conjuration** | transport creatures or objects |
| **divination** | reveal information |
| **enchantment** | influence minds |
| **evocation** | channel energy to create destructive effects |
| **illusion** | deceive the mind or senses |
| **necromancy** | manipulate life and death |
| **transmutation** | transform creatures or objects |

## Gaining Spells
You must have a spell learned, prepared, or available through a magic item to cast it.  The way you gain access to a spell determines how you can cast the spell and whether you can replace it later.

### Prepared Spells
The Spellcasting feature is the most common way for you to gain spells.  Each class prepares a different number of spells, and the Spellcasting feature describes when you can change those spells and how many you can change at a time.

Every class with a Spellcasting feature has a corresponding spell list that tells you which spells you can prepare with it.  Spells belong to all classes that can prepare them, regardless of how you gain access to them.

You can’t prepare spells that are higher level than your highest spell slot level from the class you prepare them with.

### Learned Spells
You can learn to cast spells from features from your Race, Class, or Feats.  Spells you learn from a class feature are also treated as though they were on that class’s spell list.

### Magic Items
Some Magic Items can let you cast spells through them.  Spells cast this way don't have to be learned or prepared, but still count as class spells for classes that can prepare them.

## Casting Spells
The way you cast a spell describes which Ability you use as its Spellcasting Ability.  You use your Spellcasting Ability to determine your attack modifiers and saving throw DCs for spells you cast.

### Spell Level
Every spell has a level from 0 to 6, indicated in the spell’s description.  Level 0 spells are also called Cantrips.

### Spell Slots
The Spellcasting feature gives you Spell Slots, representing your reserves of magical energy.  Spell Slots have levels from 1 to 9, corresponding to Spell Levels.  When you cast a spell with a Spell Slot, you must expend a Spell Slot of that Spell’s level or higher.

You can use Spell Slots to cast any spells you have prepared or learned.

##### One Spell Slot per Turn
You can only expend one spell slot to cast a spell each turn.  Spell slots expended for other effects don’t count against this limit.

##### Upcasting Spells
When you cast a spell using a slot that is higher level than the spell, the spell takes on the level of the slot for that casting.  Some spells have more powerful effects when cast at a higher level.

##### Casting Without a Slot
There are a few ways to cast a spell without a Spell Slot.
- **Cantrips**: Cantrips don’t expend spell slots.
- **Rituals**: Spells with the ritual tag can be cast with spell slots, or they can be cast as a ritual.  Ritual casting takes 10 minutes longer to cast than normal but doesn’t expend a spell slot.
- **Features**: Spells you learn from features sometimes also give you ways to cast them without a spell slot.
- **Magic Items**: Magic items that let you cast spells don’t expend a spell slot.  Instead, many magic items will use charges to cast spells.

### Casting Time
Casting a spell usually uses the Magic Action, but some spells require a Bonus Action, Reaction, or a longer amount of time.

##### Triggered Spells
Every spell with a Reaction casting time and some spells with a Bonus Action casting time define a trigger that must occur before you can cast the spell.

##### Long Casting Times
Some spells – including spells cast as a Ritual – require 1 minute or longer to cast.  While casting a spell this way, you must maintain Concentration and take the Magic Action on each of your turns to continue casting the spell.  If your Concentration is broken or you’re unable to take the Magic action, the spell fails but you don’t expend a spell slot.

### Components
A spell’s components are physical requirements to cast the spell.  If you can’t provide one or more of the components, you can’t cast the spell.

##### Verbal (V)
A Verbal component is the chanting of esoteric words that sound like nonsense to the uninitiated.  The words must be uttered at a normal speaking voice.

The words themselves aren’t the source of the spell’s power; rather, the combination of sounds, with specific pitch and resonance, sets the threads of magic in motion.  A creature who is gagged or in an area of magical silence can’t cast a spell with a Verbal component.

##### Somatic (S)
A Somatic component is a forceful gesticulation or an intricate set of gestures.  You must use at least one of your hands to perform these movements.

##### Material (M)
A Material component is an item used in a spell’s casting.  You must have a free hand to access a Material component, but it can be the same hand used to perform any Somatic components.

If a material component is named, a specific item must be provided.  The spell’s description states if a Material component is consumed when the spell is cast.

If a material component isn’t named, you must use a Component Pouch.  If you have the Spellcasting feature from a class the spell belongs to, you can use the Spellcasting Focus described in the feature instead.

If the spell is cast from a magic item, you use that as the spell's material component, even if the spell normally requires a named material.  Magic items used this way aren't consumed, unless their description states otherwise.

### Range
A spell’s range is usually expressed as a distance in feet or something within your Reach.  The range indicates how far from you a spell’s effect can originate – though its area or effect may be able to extend beyond this distance.

If a spell has movable effects, they aren’t restricted by the spell's range unless the description says otherwise.

### Areas of Effect
Some spells specify an area that the spell affects, which usually takes one of the following shapes.

Every shape has a point of origin from which the effect’s energy erupts in straight lines.  If a straight line from the origin to a location in the shape is blocked by Total Cover, that location is excluded from the area.

| Area | Origin | Measurements | Origin Included? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aura | the aura extends from the origin in all directions | the max radius from the origin | no, but you can choose to include it |
| Cone | the cone’s width at any point is equal to the point’s distance from the origin | the max distance from the origin | no, but you can choose to include it
| Cube | the origin can exist anywhere on a face of the cube | each side’s length | no, but you can choose to include it |
| Cylinder | the origin is the center of the cylinder’s circular top or bottom | base’s radius and cylinder’s height | yes |
| Line | the line extends from the origin | the line’s length and width | no, but you can choose to include it |
| Sphere | the sphere extends from the origin in all directions | the max radius from the origin | yes |

### Targets
Most spells require you to pick one or more targets to be affected by the spell’s magic.  A target is usually a creature or object.

##### Line of Effect
You must have a clear path to target something with a spell, so it can’t be behind Total Cover.

##### Targeting Yourself
Spells you cast with a target of Self can only target you.  A spell you cast that can target a creature can also target you, unless its description states otherwise.

##### Worn or Held Objects
Spells typically can’t target objects that are being worn or held, such as armor and weapons, even if the spell targets everything in an area.

##### Invalid Targets
If a target of a spell can’t be affected by it, nothing happens to the target, but you still cast the spell as normal.

If a spell normally has no effect on a target that succeeds on a saving throw, the invalid target appears to have succeeded on that save, giving no hint that the creature is an invalid target.

### Duration
A spell’s duration is the length of time the spell’s effect persists after it is cast.  A duration usually takes one of these forms.

- **Time Span**: The spell lasts some number of rounds, minutes, hours, or longer.  You can dismiss the spell at any time (no action required).
- **Concentration**: You must maintain concentration or the spell ends.
- **None**: The spell’s magic appears only for a moment, then disappears.

## Combining Spells
The effects of the same spell cast multiple times don’t combine.  Instead, the most potent effect from those castings applies while their durations overlap.

The effects of different spells add together while their durations overlap.

## Identifying Spells
Study Action: While an observable spell effect persists, you can make a DC 15 Arcana check.  On a success, you know the name of the spell that created the effect.

Reaction: When a creature you can see casts a spell, you can take the Study Action above.  After you do, you may use a second Reaction that triggers when the spell is cast.
