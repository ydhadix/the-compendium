# Movement

Your speed is the maximum distance you can move on your turn. Your movement can include climbing, crawling, jumping, and swimming.

## Difficult Terrain

When you move through or out of difficult terrain, each foot of movement costs 1 extra foot.

## Splitting Movement

You can move before and after any action you take on the same turn. Regardless of how many times you break up your movement, your total movement cannot exceed your speed.

## Dropping Prone

You can become prone without using an action or any of your speed, but only if your speed isn't 0.

## Other Creatures

You can freely move through the space of allies and tiny creatures. You can also move through the space of incapacitated creatures and creatures two sizes larger or smaller than you, but the space is difficult terrain for you.

You can't willingly end your move in another creature's space. If you end a turn in a space with another creature, you are knocked prone unless you are larger than the other creature or you are tiny.

## Charge

A creature is charging a target if it moves at least 20 feet in a straight line towards the target.

## Climbing

Climbing allows you to traverse vertical surfaces. You need both hands to climb and each foot of movement costs 1 extra foot. Climbing a slippery surface or one with few handholds requires a successful athletics check.

## Falling

When you are airborne and don't have a way to remain aloft, you immediately fall up to 500 feet. Until you hit the ground or gain a way to remain aloft, you continue falling 500 feet at the start of each of your turns.

At the end of the fall, you take 1d6 bludgeoning damage for every 10 feet you fell and land prone, unless you avoid taking damage from the fall.

## Jumping

Jumping allows you to ignore terrain effects when moving horizontally or vertically. Your strength determines how far or high you can jump, and each foot you jump costs one foot of movement.

If you don't move at least 10 feet in a straight line immediately before a jump, the distance you can cover is halved.

### Long Jump

When you long jump, you can cover a horizontal distance up to 10 feet, plus five times your Strength. Clearing a low obstacle requires an athletics check.

### High Jump

When you high jump, you can cover a vertical distance up to 3 feet + your Strength. You can extend your arms half your height above yourself during the jump.

## Swimming

Swimming allows you to move through liquid. You need both hands to swim and each foot of movement costs 1 extra foot. Swimming in rough waters requires a successful athletics check.

## Special Speeds

Special speeds allow you to ignore restrictions on certain kinds of movement.

If you have multiple speeds, you can switch between them as you move. The distance you travel with each speed on a turn cannot exceed that speed's maximum, and the total distance you travel on a turn cannot exceed the largest speed you used that turn.

If an effect increases or decreases your speed, any special speeds you have are also affected.
