# Initiative

Encounters are organized into cycles of rounds and turns. A round represents about 6 seconds of activity, and each participant in an encounter takes one turn in each round. Once every participant has taken a turn, the encounter continues to the next round until the encounter is resolved.

## Initiative Checks

When an encounter begins, each participant makes an Initiative Check to determine the order of turns, with the highest checks going first.

Initiative checks use your dexterity and usually don't add your proficiency.

If an initiative check is a tie, the creature with higher dexterity wins. If there's still a tie, the tied creatures each roll a d20 to determine order, highest roll going first.

## Combat

Encounters are always in Initiative order, but don't always involve fighting. Combat begins when a creature is attacked or targeted with a harmful spell or effect.

### Mounted Combat

You can ride any willing creature that has appropriate anatomy and is at least one size larger than you.

#### Mounting and Dismounting

Once per turn, you can use half of your movement to mount or dismount a creature within 5 feet of you. If your speed is 0, you can't mount or dismount.

#### Controlling a Mount

While mounted, your mount shares your turn in initiative order, though it has its own actions and movement. You can choose to control your mount or allow it to act independently. Intelligent mounts make their own decisions on how to act and may refuse being controlled.

A controlled mount moves as you direct it and can only take the Dash, Disengage, and Dodge actions.

An independent mount moves and acts as it wishes with no restrictions on its actions.

#### Staying Mounted

If an effect moves you against your will while mounted, you are dismounted.

If an effect moves your mount against its will or you are knocked prone while mounted, you must make a DC 10 reflex save. On a failed save, you are dismounted and land Prone within 5 feet of your mount. On a successful save, you remain mounted and not Prone.

If your mount is knocked prone, you can use your reaction to dismount safely. Otherwise, you are dismounted and land Prone within 5 feet of your mount.
